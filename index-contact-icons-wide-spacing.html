About Me

I am a biophysics researcher focused on developing practical optical technologies for cancer detection, treatment monitoring, and photodynamic therapy (PDT). My work combines fluorescence spectroscopy, biomedical optics, device engineering, biological validation, and technology commercialization to make advanced photomedicine more accessible, particularly in resource-limited settings.

My current work spans PpIX/PPP photophysics, dual-wavelength PDT, cutaneous photosensitizer detection, smartphone fluorescence imaging, and AI-assisted PDT research workflows. I am particularly interested in translating laboratory-scale optical systems into compact, low-cost platforms that can support bedside, outpatient, and global-health applications.

ACTIVE RESEARCH PROJECT

Dual PDT: PpIX and its chlorin photoproducts

Overview

Topical or systemic 5-aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) administration leads to intracellular accumulation of protoporphyrin IX (PpIX), which is then excited with red light (typically 630–635 nm) to generate reactive oxygen species that mediate tumor cell death in photodynamic therapy (PDT). PpIX is strongly photolabile in biological tissue, and photobleaching can correlate with PDT-induced tissue effects.

During irradiation, PpIX can be partially converted to a chlorin-type photoproduct, commonly termed photoprotoporphyrin (PPP). The project explores a two-stage PDT concept: an initial PpIX-dominated phase under 630–635 nm excitation followed by a photoproduct-dominated phase using longer-wavelength excitation around 670–675 nm.

Research Concept

Sequential illumination at approximately 635 nm followed by approximately 670 nm is being investigated as a way to exploit photoproduct photosensitizing activity and potentially extend the therapeutic window without increasing photosensitizer dose.

Research Direction

  • PpIX photobleaching and PPP formation
  • Sequential wavelength illumination
  • Fluorescence monitoring of PpIX and photoproduct signals
  • Dosimetry-oriented interpretation of photobleaching and photoproduct accumulation
  • Portable fiber-coupled light delivery

BIOPHYSICS RESEARCH

Photosensitizer Detection

Research Focus

Although PDT offers lower off-target toxicity than many cancer treatments, systemic photosensitization can cause cutaneous phototoxicity. The project addresses post-treatment monitoring by developing the Cutaneous Photosensitivity Detection (CPD) device for measuring cutaneous accumulation of protoporphyrin IX (PpIX) or Photofrin®.

The prototype integrates a 405 nm LED excitation source, a 600 nm long-pass optical filter, and a photodiode sensor housed within a custom 3D-printed enclosure. A reflectance-based correction method is used to normalize fluorescence intensity and compensate for skin-tone-dependent attenuation.

Validation Approach

  • Standardized PpIX solutions
  • Hydrogel phantoms
  • ALA-induced 3D cancer cell cultures
  • Murine models
  • Comparison with established fluorescence imaging systems

Key Findings

The old portfolio describes validation across phantom, cell-culture, and animal models, with high sensitivity in the micromolar to sub-micromolar range and agreement with measurements from a commercial in vivo imaging platform.

Future Directions

Development is continuing toward patient-specific melanin attenuation correction that can be estimated and applied in real time, with the goal of improving PpIX quantification across diverse skin tones.

INNOVATION PROJECT

Smartphone Fluorescence Imaging

Concept

Developing a smartphone-based fluorescence imaging system for real-time treatment monitoring and tumor-margin visualization during PDT, with the goal of making fluorescence-based diagnostic capabilities accessible without expensive specialized equipment.

Technical Approach

  • 405 nm blue LED excitation attachment for smartphones
  • Custom optical filters to capture PpIX fluorescence
  • Machine-learning algorithms for automated tumor detection
  • Real-time image processing on mobile devices
  • Cloud-based data storage and analysis platform

Clinical Applications

The old portfolio describes potential use for visualizing photosensitizer distribution, assessing treatment completeness through photobleaching, and maintaining treatment records using devices already available to clinicians.

Validation & Status

The previous portfolio describes pilot validation against professional fluorescence imaging systems and a commercialization pathway involving smartphone and medical-device partnerships. These claims should be updated to the latest verified project status before public launch.
